## 1. Substrate Types and Characteristics

Wood siding materials are classified by extractive chemistry and durability:

### Extractive-Rich Woods (Water-Soluble Extractives - WSE)
- **Species**: Western Red Cedar, Redwood, Cypress
- Natural decay resistance driven by high concentrations of water-soluble tannins
- Tannins dissolve in moisture and bleed through finishes — highly problematic for coatings

### Resinous Woods (Solvent-Soluble Extractives - SSE)
- **Species**: Pine, Spruce, Douglas Fir
- Contain pitch and resin (non-water-soluble extractives)
- Liquefy and exude from wood (especially at knots) when exposed to high temperatures

### High-Density Hardwoods
- **Species**: Ipe, Teak, Garapa
- Extreme durability but highly dense
- Require specialized preparation and fastening systems

### Profile Geometries

| Profile | Description | Special Considerations |
|---------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Lap / Clapboard** | Horizontal overlapping profiles | Effectively shed water; prone to capillary "lap staining" where overlapping boards trap moisture |
| **Board and Batten / T&G** | Vertical and interlocking systems | Demand pre-finishing on all six sides before installation; shrinkage exposes unpainted wood. Board and batten increases paintable surface area by 15-25% |
| **Shingles and Shakes** | Sawn or hand-split profiles | Highest geometric complexity; massive effective surface areas; extreme capillary wicking potential |

## 2. Surface Preparation Protocols by Condition

### New Wood Construction
- **Mill glaze**: Hard, glossy layer of crushed wood cells/resins from high-speed planer blades — prevents primer penetration
- **Removal**: Sand with 80-120 grit sandpaper
- **Fasteners**: Must be stainless steel or hot-dipped galvanized, countersunk, spot-primed, and filled to prevent iron-staining

### Weathered Bare Wood
- UV-degraded "silver-gray" fibers lack structural integrity — painting over them = instant peeling
- Sand or power-scrub to expose fresh wood
- **High-pressure washing discouraged**: Damages springwood, causes "fuzzy" washboard texture

### Sound Existing Paint
- Wash with TSP solution to remove oxidized pigment (chalk) and dirt
- Light sanding or "liquid sanders" to dull glossy finishes and provide mechanical tooth

### Failed (Peeling/Cracking) Paint
- Remove all flaking paint via carbide hand scrapers or HEPA-equipped paint shavers
- "Feather-sand" edges of remaining sound paint to prevent visible mapping
- Spot-prime all bare spots

## 3. Primer and Coating System Selection

### Standard System: 1+2
One full coat of primer (1.5-2.2 mils DFT) + two topcoats (1.5-2.0 mils DFT each) = total system thickness 4.5-6.2 mils.

### Primers

| Substrate Chemistry | Required Primer | Notes |
|-------------------|----------------|-------|
| Tannin-rich (Cedar, Redwood) | Stain-blocking primer (alkyd or advanced acrylic tannin-blocker) | Mandatory — locks in extractives |
| Heavily weathered wood | Deep-penetrating oil-alkyd primer | Binds loose fibers best |
| Standard softwoods | Acrylic stain-blocking primer | VOC-compliant alternative |

### Topcoats
- **100% acrylic latex** superior to alkyds — retains flexibility for wood expansion/contraction, resists cracking

### Film-Forming Paint vs. Solid Stain

| Property | Film-Forming Paint | Solid-Color Stain |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| UV protection | Maximum | Good |
| Hide imperfections | Yes | Moderate |
| Service life | 8-12 years | 3-8 years |
| Vapor permeability | Low (vapor barrier) | High (breathable) |
| Failure mode | Blisters if moisture enters | Gradual fade, no peeling |
| Best for | Dry climates, smooth profiles | High-moisture climates, textured profiles |

## 4. Production Rates by Substrate and Method

Spraying wood siding universally requires back-brushing or back-rolling for fiber penetration. Multi-story ladder work reduces base rates by 20-40%.

| Surface Type | Airless Spray (SF/hr) | Brush and Roll (SF/hr) |
|-------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Smooth lap siding | 150-250 | 80-120 |
| Board and batten | 120-180 | 50-70 (brush only) |
| Wood shingles/shakes | 100-150 | 30-50 (brush only) |
| Window/door trim | — | 40-60 LF/hr (brush only) |

## 5. Failure Modes Specific to Wood Siding

### Extractive Bleeding
- **Run-down streaking**: Liquid water gets behind siding and washes tannins out through laps (poor back-priming)
- **Diffused staining**: Moisture vapor migrates through weak paint film

### Iron Staining
- Blue-black halos around nail heads
- Chemical reaction between water, wood tannins, and non-galvanized/non-stainless fasteners

### Pitch/Knot Bleeding
- Yellowish/orange resin oozing through paint film
- Driven by heat (sunlight) drawing turpentine/rosin from pine or fir knots

### Moisture Blisters vs. Temperature Blisters

| Type | Contents | Cause | Timing |
|------|----------|-------|--------|
| Moisture blisters | Liquid water | Vapor migrating from inside house or entering unsealed joints | Develop over time |
| Temperature blisters | Air/vapor | Oil-based paint applied in direct sun; top-layer skins trapping solvents | During/shortly after application |

### Cross-Grain Cracking
- Deep cracking perpendicular to wood grain
- Caused by excessive paint thickness buildup from decades of over-painting

## 6. Moisture Management Requirements

### Moisture Content Constraints

| Threshold | MC Value | Consequence |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| Maximum permissible for painting | 15% | — |
| Guaranteed failure | >20% | Catastrophic coating failure |
| Fiber Saturation Point (FSP) | 25-30% | Free water in cell cavities |

- Wood must be acclimated to equilibrium on-site before installation

### Envelope Sealing

| Sealing Action | Purpose | Impact |
|---------------|---------|--------|
| **Back-priming** | Coat hidden rear face of siding | Stops interior vapor condensation from cupping board and washing out tannins |
| **End-sealing** | Seal cut ends | End-grain wicks water 10-20x faster than flat grain |

### Structural Defenses
- Ice dams, lack of vapor barriers, and missing kick-out flashing routinely inject bulk water behind cladding
- These obliterate exterior coatings regardless of paint quality

## 7. Maintenance and Recoat Protocols

### Cleaning
- Annual washing prevents dirt and mold buildup
- WSE tannin stains: scrub with soap/water or oxalic acid solution
- Iron stains: oxalic acid required to neutralize blue-black discoloration

### Pitch Bleed Remediation
1. Scrape hardened pitch (or dissolve with mineral spirits if still soft)
2. Spot-prime knot with alcohol-borne shellac or dedicated knot sealer
3. Blocks further solvent-soluble extractive migration

### Recoating Guidelines
- Do not recoat too frequently — prevents cross-grain cracking
- Maintenance coats should be applied thin
- Wood must maintain vapor permeability
