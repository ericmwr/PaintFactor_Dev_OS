## 1. Exterior Paint Film Failure Modes

Each failure mode provides diagnostic evidence for root cause — poor application, environmental assault, or substrate instability.

### UV-Driven Degradation

| Mode | Mechanism | Visual Diagnosis | Remediation |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Chalking** | UV breaks down resin binders, releasing pigment as powder | Powdery transfer to touch/dark cloth | Thorough removal or chalk-binding primer |
| **Oxidation** | Alkyd/oil binders react with atmospheric oxygen, becoming hard and brittle | Dullness, loss of sheen, color lightening | Sand to sound surface and recoat |

- Chalking is more prevalent in low-quality, highly pigmented paints
- Oxidation is accelerated in high-altitude/high-UV zones
- Minor chalking in light colors can be intentional ("self-cleaning" effect); excessive chalking = film thinning

### Mechanical / Flexibility Failures

| Mode | Severity | Description | Primary Cause |
|------|----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Checking** | Minor | Long, evenly-spaced shallow cracks | Aged multi-layer oil paint hardened beyond flex capacity |
| **Splitting / Cracking** | Moderate | Fracture through full coat(s), may expose substrate | Insufficient drying, structural movement, wood seasoning cracks |
| **Alligatoring** | Catastrophic | Deep interconnected cracks (alligator-hide pattern) | Intercoat incompatibility — hard coating over soft/flexible base |

- Wood seasoning checks transmit stress directly through paint film along grain
- Alligatoring: classic cause is oil enamel over latex primer

### Adhesion Failures

| Mode | Mechanism | Diagnostic Clue |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Peeling** | Surface contamination (dust, grease, chalk) prevents bond; or intercoat failure on glossy surfaces | Large ribbons/flakes detaching |
| **Blistering (heat)** | Applied in direct sun — surface skins while solvents vaporize beneath | Bubbles that appear during/shortly after application |
| **Blistering (moisture)** | Vapor pressure from within substrate pushes film off | Bubbles that develop over time; water inside blisters |

## 2. Bond Failure Classification

The location of fracture identifies the weakest link in the coating assembly.

| Failure Type | Fracture Location | Evidence | Root Cause |
|-------------|-------------------|----------|------------|
| **Adhesive** | Interface between paint and substrate (or between coats) | Substrate left bare, no residue | Surface contamination, low surface energy substrate |
| **Cohesive** | Within the paint film itself | Paint residue on both separated surfaces | Over-thinning, excessive film thickness, poor mixing |
| **Substrate** | Substrate material breaks | Wood fibers on back of paint chips ("fiber tear") | Rot, moisture degradation, compromised substrate |

**Weak Boundary Layer (WBL) Theory**: Failures at the interface are often caused by a thin fragile layer (dust, moisture, hydrolytic primer breakdown) preventing coating-substrate contact. Surface preparation = systematic removal of WBLs.

## 3. Field Adhesion Testing Protocols

### ASTM D3359 — Tape Adhesion Test

**Method A (X-Cut)** — Primary field method for coatings > 5 mils:
1. Sharp razor makes X-shaped cut (30-45 degree intersection angle) through film to substrate
2. Pressure-sensitive tape applied, rubbed firmly with eraser
3. Tape removed rapidly within 90 seconds at 180 degree angle

| Rating | Result | Interpretation |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| **5A** | No peeling or removal | Excellent — ideal for overcoating |
| **4A** | Trace peeling along incisions | Good — overcoat after standard prep |
| **3A** | Jagged removal up to 1/16" (1.6mm) | Marginal — requires spot priming |
| **2A** | Jagged removal up to 1/8" (3.2mm) | Fail — significant delamination risk |
| **1A** | Removal from most of X area | Poor — entire system needs removal |
| **0A** | Removal beyond incisions | Critical fail — total system removal |

**Method B (Cross-Hatch)** — For thin coatings < 5 mils, lab settings. Grid of 6-11 perpendicular cuts. Limited value on textured surfaces (stucco, EIFS).

### ASTM D4541 — Pull-Off Test
- Loading fixture (dolly) glued to surface, portable tester applies perpendicular tensile force
- Records actual bond strength in psi/MPa
- More objective and repeatable than tape test
- Used for critical infrastructure requiring numerical bond strength

## 4. Moisture Content Thresholds

### Maximum MC Before Coating Application

| Substrate | Target MC | Critical/Saturated MC |
|-----------|-----------|----------------------|
| Interior woodwork / cabinetry | 6-8% | > 12% |
| Exterior wood siding | 12-15% | > 19% |
| Concrete / masonry | < 12% | > 15% |
| Stucco | < 12% | > 12% |
| Gypsum board | < 12% | — |

- Wood above 19-20% MC is at high risk for fungal growth and structural decay
- **Fiber Saturation Point (FSP)**: ~25-30% MC. Above FSP, free water in cell cavities (no dimensional change). Below FSP, bound water lost from cell walls = shrinkage
- Painting wood above service EMC = wood shrinks as it dries = splitting/checking of paint film

### On-Site Measurement Tools

| Tool | Method | Best For |
|------|--------|----------|
| **Pin-type meter** | Electrical resistance between inserted pins | Point-specific depth readings, finding hidden moisture pockets |
| **Pinless meter** | Non-invasive electromagnetic field scan | Rapid broad-area screening (sensitive to surface flatness) |
| **ASTM D4263 plastic sheet test** | 18" square plastic taped for 16 hours | Concrete walls — visible condensation = moisture migration |

### Environmental Conditions During Application
- Relative humidity must be below 85%
- Dew point variance: minimum 5 degrees F (3 degrees C) between air and surface temperature

## 5. Substrate Condition Classification

### MPI Degree of Surface Degradation (DSD)

| Level | Name | Description | Preparation Required |
|-------|------|-------------|---------------------|
| **DSD-0** | Sound Surface | No visible damage; cosmetic changes only | Cleaning only |
| **DSD-1** | Slightly Deteriorated | Minor fading, chalking, light scratches; primer intact | Power wash, spot clean, treat mildew |
| **DSD-2** | Moderately Deteriorated | Localized peeling/flaking/staining; primer may not be intact | Scrape/sand, power tool clean, patch, dull glossy finishes, spot prime |
| **DSD-3** | Severely Deteriorated | Heavy peeling, deep cracks, widespread failure | Abrasive blast, heavy grinding, extensive repair, full system prime |
| **DSD-4** | Substrate Damage | Wood rot, concrete spalling, metal corrosion | Structural repair/replacement mandatory before any coating |

### NPS Paint Surface Conditions

| Class | Severity | Action |
|-------|----------|--------|
| **Class I** | Minor blemishes, dirt, mildew, surface chalking | No paint removal required |
| **Class II** | Top layer failure, crazing | Limited paint removal |
| **Class III** | Substantial multi-layer failure, deep cracking, alligatoring | Total paint removal |

## 6. PCA Standards for Surface Preparation

### PCA P14 — Levels of Surface Preparation

> **Default: If contract does not specify, Level 2 applies.**

| Level | Name | Focus | Key Activities | Recommended For |
|-------|------|-------|----------------|-----------------|
| **1** | Basic | Cleanliness for adhesion; appearance not a concern | Wash, light power wash, hand clean, remove obvious loose paint, treat mildew | DSD-0, DSD-1 |
| **2** | Standard | Adhesion + longevity; appearance secondary | All Level 1 + solvent clean, basic patch/fill, caulk, light sand/abrade, feather-edge sand. Defects > 1/8" corrected. Tape adhesion test recommended | DSD-0, DSD-1, DSD-2 |
| **3** | Superior | Detailed preparation for premium finish | All Level 2 + thorough patching, detailed sanding. Defects > 1/16" eliminated | Premium residential, new construction |
| **4** | Supreme | Cosmetic perfection | All Level 3 + fill/sand variations > 1/32". Smooth to "touch and feel" | High-end trim, doors, handrails |
| **R** | Restoration | Severely deteriorated surfaces | Complete or near-complete coating removal (abrasion, chemical, heat). Substrate repair/replacement | DSD-3, DSD-4 |

### PCA P1 — Properly Painted Surface
- Uniform in appearance, color, texture, and sheen
- Free of runs, sags, holidays, drips, splatters, overspray
- **Inspection distance**: 39 inches (1 meter), no magnification, under finished lighting

### MPI RSP (Repaint Surface Preparation) Standards

| Standard | Method | Application |
|----------|--------|-------------|
| RSP-1 | Hand cleaning (scrapers, wire brushes) | Remove non-adherent contaminants |
| RSP-2 | Solvent cleaning | Metals — remove drawing compounds/oils |
| RSP-5 | Pressure washing | Soft wash (100-600 psi) for cedar/soft stone; high pressure for masonry |
| RSP-8 | Efflorescence removal (muriatic acid) | Brick/stucco salt deposits |
| RSP-12 | Rust and cedar stain treatment (oxalic acid) | Metallic oxides, wood tannin extractives |

## 7. Chalking Remediation Protocols

Chalking residue is a primary "bond-breaker" causing premature peeling if painted over.

### Cleaning Hierarchy

| Severity | Method | Post-Treatment |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| **Light** | Low-pressure rinse + soft brush + mild detergent | Verify with hand-rub test |
| **Moderate** | Wire brushing or mechanical sanding to sound coating + high-volume water rinse | Re-test after 24-48 hours drying |
| **Heavy/Severe** | Controlled pressure washing or abrasive blasting | If runoff still milky or powder remains: second cycle or binding primer |

### Chalk-Binding Primers
- Used when 100% chalk removal would damage substrate (stucco, masonry)
- Low-viscosity, high-penetration resins soak through powder and lock it to stable substrate
- Example: Loxon Acrylic Conditioner
- Essential in high-UV environments (Florida, Colorado, high altitude)

## 8. Production Rate Multipliers by Surface Deterioration

### Preparation Labor Multipliers

| Condition | DSD Rating | Description | Prep Multiplier |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Good** | DSD-0/1 | Standard prep (wash, light scrape) | 1.0x - 1.25x (baseline) |
| **Fair** | DSD-2 | Moderate prep (extensive scrape/fill) | 1.25x - 1.5x (+25-50%) |
| **Poor** | DSD-3 | Extensive prep (stripping, full prime) | 1.5x - 2.0x (+50-100%) |
| **Failed** | DSD-4 | Structural damage / system removal | 3.0x+ or T&M |

> "Remediation typically costs three times more than proper initial application."

### Logistical Multipliers (Applied on top of deterioration multiplier)

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Scaffolding / aerial lifts | -10% to -35% effective production |
| Metro area labor premium | +15-30% total project cost |
| Adverse weather (humidity/wind) | -10-15% productivity |

### Labor Estimation Formula

```
Effective Production Rate = Baseline Rate / (DM x CM)
```

Where DM = Deterioration Multiplier, CM = Complexity/Access Multiplier.

**Example**: Siding baseline 150 SF/hr, Poor condition (DM=1.75), boom lift (CM=1.20):
- Effective rate = 150 / (1.75 x 1.20) = ~71 SF/hr (nearly double the labor cost)

### Defect Cost Weighting (for severity indexing)

| Anomaly | Cost Weight |
|---------|-------------|
| Stains / color change | 0.25 |
| Cracking | 1.00 |
| Chalking | 1.00 |
| Loss of adherence | 1.50 |

## 9. Service Life Reference Data

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Mean service life of exterior paint | 9.5-10 years |
| With routine maintenance | 15-16 years |
| Key degradation variables | Distance from sea, wind-rain action, orientation (N vs S) |

**Multi-Repaint Risk**: Successive coats reduce water vapor permeance, potentially converting "breather" coatings into vapor barriers. WUFI modeling shows trapped moisture can reach 100% RH, causing interior mold, exterior blistering, and masonry spalling.

### Orientation Effects on Failure Modes
- **South/West facades**: Chalking, cracking, loss of adherence (UV + heat)
- **North facades**: Staining from dampness

## PaintFactor Integration Notes

### Vocabulary Distinction: Substrate State vs. Substrate Condition

**Substrate State** (`SS_*`) describes the *existing coating/finish status* of the surface — what has been applied to it. It determines the prep pathway and primer selection:
- `SS_BARE` — Uncoated raw substrate
- `SS_PAINTED` — Previously painted
- `SS_STAINED` — Previously stained (transparent or solid)
- `SS_FACTORY_PRIMED` — Manufacturer-applied primer (e.g., Hardie, LP SmartSide)
- `SS_FACTORY_FINISHED` — Manufacturer-applied finish coat (e.g., ColorPlus, ExpertFinish)
- `SS_CLEAR_SEALED` — Clear sealer or varnish applied

**Substrate Condition** (`SC_*`) describes the *level of deterioration* of the surface, mapped to MPI DSD ratings. It determines the prep labor multiplier and preparation level:
- `SC_SOUND` = DSD-0 (no prep beyond cleaning)
- `SC_SLIGHT_DETERIORATION` = DSD-1 (minor fading, chalking, light scratches)
- `SC_MODERATE_DETERIORATION` = DSD-2 (localized peeling/flaking/staining)
- `SC_SEVERE_DETERIORATION` = DSD-3 (heavy peeling, deep cracks, widespread failure)
- `SC_SUBSTRATE_DAMAGE` = DSD-4 (rot, spalling, corrosion — structural)

These are independent dimensions: a surface can be `SS_PAINTED` + `SC_SOUND` (good existing paint) or `SS_PAINTED` + `SC_SEVERE_DETERIORATION` (failing paint system). Both values are required for correct spec activation and labor estimation.

### Estimation Engine Implications
- Substrate condition maps to prep labor multiplier table (Section 8)
- Substrate state determines primer requirement (bare = mandatory prime; painted + sound = paint-on-paint may be acceptable)
- Access/complexity multipliers stack multiplicatively with deterioration multipliers
- Chalking remediation is a distinct prep task with its own production rate
- Adhesion testing (ASTM D3359) result informs both condition rating and primer decision

### Spec Activation Rules
- SC_SEVERE_DETERIORATION (DSD-3+) requires Restoration prep level (PCA P14 Level R)
- SC_SUBSTRATE_DAMAGE (DSD-4) requires structural repair scope — outside painting spec boundary
- Moisture content must be validated before any prep or coating spec activates
