# PAINTFACTOR DOCTRINE: Painted Doors - Residential

> **Scope:** This doctrine governs painted door specifications for residential applications including interior flush, interior panel, exterior entry, French doors, and bifold doors. Applies to new construction and repaint scenarios.

> **Critical - PaintScope Integration:** Door specs consume geometry from PaintScope. Specs must NOT compute quantities - only reference surface types and apply labor/material factors.

### 1.2 Measurement Conventions

> **PaintFactor Domain Knowledge:** The standard convention is per side. This eliminates the need for special conventions and provides consistent estimating units.

| Convention | Definition | Use Case |
|------------|------------|----------|
| **EA (per side)** | One face of door including edges | **Standard bidding unit** |
| EA (per door) | Complete door, both sides, all edges | Legacy/alternate |
| SF | Square footage (~17.5 SF per side) | Large/unusual doors |

### 1.3 Interior vs Exterior Classification

| Attribute | Interior | Exterior |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Coating system | Standard latex/waterborne alkyd | UV-resistant, flexible coatings |
| Prep intensity | Lower | Higher (weathering assessment) |
| Edge treatment | Standard | Critical (moisture barrier) |
| Quality floor | QT2 acceptable | QT3 minimum; QT2 w/ disclaimer only |
| DFT requirement | 2.0-3.0 mils | 3.0-4.0 mils |
| Color restrictions | None | Dark colors increase thermal stress |

> **Note:** Exterior doors at QT2 require customer acknowledgment of reduced warranty coverage due to durability limitations.

## 2. Substrate Classification

### 2.1 Substrate Types

| Substrate | Applications | Key Considerations |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------|
| Solid wood (pine, poplar) | Premium interior, entry | Grain filling, knot sealing required |
| MDF (molded panel) | Interior paint-grade | Edge sealing critical; no grain/knots |
| Hollow core | Budget interior | Light sanding only; fragile |
| Fiberglass (smooth) | Exterior entry | Adhesion primer required; warp-resistant |
| Fiberglass (textured) | Exterior, wood-look | Can be stained or painted |
| Steel (20-24 gauge) | Exterior, fire-rated | Rust prevention critical |
| Composite | Newer exterior options | Per manufacturer specs |

### 2.2 Factory Primed Assessment

| Condition | Required Action |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Intact, clean | Scuff sand 180-grit, proceed (field primer recommended) |
| Damaged/scratched | Spot prime damaged areas, sand blend |
| Weathered/chalking | Full sand, full prime |
| Unknown origin | Test adhesion, bonding primer if doubtful |

> **Critical:** Factory primer is transit protection, not finish-ready primer. Always assess and typically apply field primer for quality work.

## 3. Surface Preparation Requirements

### 3.1 New Construction - By Substrate

| Substrate | Cleaning | Sanding | Sealing | Notes |
|-----------|----------|---------|---------|-------|
| Solid wood | Dust removal | 150-grit | Shellac for knots | Grain raise risk |
| MDF | Dust removal | 120-150 edges | Seal all edges | Edges porous |
| Hollow core | Dust removal | Light 180 | Standard | No heavy pressure |
| Fiberglass | Degreaser | Scuff pad | Bonding primer | Remove mold-release |
| Steel | Degreaser | 120-150 | DTM primer | Apply immediately |

### 3.2 Repaint Preparation

| Existing Condition | Required Prep |
|-------------------|---------------|
| Sound paint, good adhesion | Clean with TSP/degreaser, scuff sand 150-220 grit |
| Peeling/flaking | Scrape loose, feather edges, spot prime |
| Multiple coats (buildup) | Assess for stripping or heavy sand |
| Unknown coating | Adhesion test, bonding primer if needed |
| Gloss/semi-gloss | Deglosser or thorough sand for tooth |

### 3.3 Contaminant Removal

Doors are high-touch surfaces accumulating oils, cleaning chemicals, and dust. Failure to remove contaminants causes fisheying or premature delamination.

1. Wash with TSP solution or specialized degreaser
2. Rinse with clean water
3. Allow complete drying (TSP residue interferes with acrylic curing)

## 4. Primer Systems by Substrate

| Substrate | Primer Type | Purpose | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|---------|-------|
| Solid wood | Stain-blocking primer | Seal, block tannins | Shellac for knots |
| MDF (edges) | Shellac or oil-based | Seal porous edges | Water causes swelling |
| MDF (faces) | High-build latex | Build film, level | After edge seal |
| Hollow core | Standard latex | Seal, provide tooth | Light application |
| Fiberglass | Bonding primer | Adhesion to slick surface | Remove mold-release first |
| Steel | DTM primer | Rust inhibition | Apply immediately |

### 4.1 Primer Functions

1. **Seal the substrate** - Prevent uneven absorption
2. **Provide tooth** - Mechanical adhesion for topcoat
3. **Block stains/tannins** - Prevent bleed-through

## 5. Finish Coat Systems

### 5.1 Interior Door Coatings

| Coating Type | Benefits | Limitations | Use |
|--------------|----------|-------------|-----|
| Standard Latex | Fast dry, low odor | Lower hardness, blocking | QT2 budget |
| Waterborne Alkyd | Excellent leveling, hard | Longer cure time | QT3-QT4 standard |
| Urethane-Modified | Superior durability | Higher cost | High-traffic, QT4+ |
| Water-Based Urethane | Hardness of oil | More expensive | Kitchen/bath |

### 5.2 Exterior Door Requirements

- UV resistance: Titanium dioxide pigmentation; UV absorbers
- Flexibility: Accommodate substrate movement
- Moisture barrier: Sealed edges, adequate DFT
- Total DFT: 3.0-4.0 mils minimum

### 5.3 Sheen Recommendations

| Sheen | Use | Durability | Touch-Up | Notes |
|-------|-----|------------|----------|-------|
| Satin | Standard interior | Good | Moderate | Most forgiving |
| Semi-gloss | Traditional, kitchens | Very good | Moderate | Shows defects |
| Gloss | High-end, entry | Excellent | Difficult | Requires Level 4 prep |

### 5.4 Blocking Prevention

Traditional latex paints are often too soft for doors, causing blocking (door sticks to weatherstripping or frame). Use urethane-alkyd hybrids, water-based urethanes, or higher resin-to-pigment ratio products.

## 7. Door Handling & Setup Methods

> **PaintFactor Domain Knowledge:** Setup method is contractor discretion based on project conditions - batch efficiency, ergonomics, available space, logistics. NOT a quality tier selection. METHOD DOES NOT CORRELATE TO QUALITY TIER.

### 7.1 Method Overview

| Method | Setup Intensity | Primary Considerations |
|--------|-----------------|------------------------|
| In-Frame (On-Hinge) | Baseline | Any quantity, any color scenario, most common |
| Chevron Stand (Vertical) | Medium | Batch processing, doors removed |
| Horizontal Handle Rack | High | Ergonomic prep, requires spray + drying areas |

### 7.2 In-Frame Method (On-Hinge) - BASELINE

**When Used:**
- Any number of doors - frequently used for entire houses
- Same OR different color/finish as frames
- Spray, brush, or roll application (spray requires experienced painter)

#### Color/Finish Scenarios

| Scenario | Application | Masking Requirement |
|----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Same color as frame | Brush/roll | Minimal |
| Same color as frame | Spray | Standard overspray protection |
| Different color than frame | Brush/roll | Minimal (cut in) |
| Different color than frame | Spray | Mask door OR frame (whichever painted first) |

#### Hinge Handling Options

| Option | Method | Time per Hinge |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| A | Mask hinges | ~5 min |
| B | Swap to dummy hinges (pre-painted sacrificial set) | ~5 min |

**Dummy Hinge System:** Maintain set of matching dummy hinges per common size/style. Dummy hinges accumulate paint over time (acceptable). Clean hinges stored safely during painting. Swap back after final coat cures.

#### Time Factors

| Task | Time | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| Floor protection | Shared | Across doors in room |
| Hinge handling (mask or swap) | ~15 min/door | ~5 min x 3 hinges typical |
| Hinge swap back to clean | ~15 min/door | Option B only |

> **Note:** Larger doors or doors with more than 3 hinges increase hinge time proportionally.

### 7.3 Chevron Stand Method (Vertical)

**When Used:** Contractor preference for batch processing. Doors removed and staged vertically.

> **Critical:** Note door location on top of door or on hinge seat before removal.

**Setup Sequence:**
1. Remove door from frame (note hinge side, swing direction, mark location)
2. **Remove hardware if installed - bag and label**
3. Install buttons on door bottom (or use shims)
4. Stand doors at angles, connect with L-brackets (chevron pattern from above)
5. Prep, prime, paint all accessible surfaces
6. Allow dry time
7. Disconnect brackets, return to field for install

#### Chevron Method Time Factors

| Task | Time per Door |
|------|---------------|
| Hardware removal | ~20 min |
| Prop up with buttons/shims, stand and install brackets | 2-5 min |
| Remove brackets and relocate door | 2-5 min |
| Reinstall hardware, install door | 10-20 min |

### 7.4 Horizontal Handle Rack Method

**When Used:** Contractor preference for ergonomic prep (waist-level work reduces labor intensity). Requires adequate space for spray area AND separate drying/staging area.

> **Critical:** Note door location on top of door or on hinge seat before removal.

**Advantages:**
- Prep at waist level - less labor intensive
- Doors dry perfectly flat (no sag, no drip migration)
- Stackable during dry time (handles act as spacers)

**Disadvantages:**
- Highest setup time
- Requires significant space (work area + separate drying area)
- Handle install/removal adds labor

#### Horizontal Method Time Factors

| Task | Time per Door |
|------|---------------|
| Hardware removal (same as chevron) | ~20 min |
| Install handles/brackets | 5-10 min |
| Uninstall handles/brackets | 5-10 min |
| Reinstall hardware, install door | 10-20 min |

## 8. Application Methods

### 8.1 Brush and Roller Technique

In occupied residential settings, brushing and rolling remain practical methods. Can be used for any number of doors when spraying is not an option or not preferred.

**Panel Door Sequence (PCA recommended):**
1. Panels first: Cut in edges with brush, fill flat areas with small roller
2. Horizontal rails: Tie in brush marks to panels
3. Vertical stiles: Continuous vertical pattern
4. Edges: Edge visible when door open matches adjacent face

**Coat Requirements:** Two coats high-quality semi-gloss or satin enamel for full hiding and durability.

### 8.2 Spray Application

Preferred for new construction or high-end renovations requiring factory-grade finish.

| Spray Type | Use Case | Overspray | Control |
|------------|----------|-----------|---------|
| HVLP | Interior doors, fine finish | Lower | Higher |
| Airless | High-volume production | Higher | Lower |

**Primary Advantage:** Absence of brush/roller marks; perfectly smooth, uniform film.

> **PaintFactor Domain Knowledge:** Spray feasibility depends on project context. Full interior repaints benefit from spray as an option when protection is already in place. In occupied homes where brush and roll is primary application with minimal protection, spray doors is not feasible due to excessive masking requirements unless specifically requested by customer.

### 8.3 Cycle Time Comparison

| Factor | Hand-Painting (In Situ) | Spray (Off-Site/Area) |
|--------|------------------------|----------------------|
| Masking/Setup | 10-20 mins per door | 60-90 mins per area |
| Application (2 coats) | 40-60 mins per door | 15-20 mins per door |
| Drying/Flip Time | 4-6 hours | 1-2 hours (with fans) |
| Cleanup | 10 mins | 30-45 mins |
| Best For | Occupied homes; any quantity if spray not feasible | New construction; 10+ doors |

## 11. Common Defects & Mitigation

| Defect | Cause | Prevention | Remediation |
|--------|-------|------------|-------------|
| Runs/sags | Over-application | Proper mil thickness, light coats | Sand flat, recoat |
| Orange peel | Wrong tip/pressure | Adjust equipment, distance | Sand 320+, recoat |
| Brush marks | Wrong brush, technique | Quality brush, lay-off strokes | Sand, respray |
| Edge failures | Poor edge seal (MDF) | Seal edges before prime | Strip, seal, reprime |
| Grain telegraph | Insufficient fill | Multiple fill/sand cycles | Sand back, fill, recoat |
| Adhesion failure | Inadequate prep | Proper prep, adhesion primer | Strip, start over |
| Blocking | Soft coating | Use urethane-alkyd, full cure | Recoat harder enamel |
| Surfactant leach | Low temp/high humidity | Apply in proper conditions | Wash off, allow cure |
| Fisheying | Contamination | Thorough cleaning, TSP wash | Strip, clean, restart |

### 11.1 Environmental Application Windows

| Condition | Minimum | Maximum | Notes |
|-----------|---------|---------|-------|
| Temperature | 50°F (10°C) | 90°F (32°C) | Water-based coatings |
| Humidity | 20% RH | 85% RH | Affects cure time |
| Direct sunlight | Avoid | Avoid | Rapid dry, poor leveling |

## 12. Specialty Door Considerations

### 12.1 Bifold Doors
- Mask track when spraying in place or spraying frame
- Remove or mask track rollers on door when spraying/staging

### 12.2 Louver Doors
> **PaintFactor Domain Knowledge:** Better to spray 3 light finish coats on louver doors than 2 normal coats. Risk of drips on louvered portion is high, and sanding/removal takes extremely long time.

### 12.3 Multi-Color Doors (Different Color Per Side)
Edge work masking on leaf edge. Standard mask and cut rates apply.

### 12.4 Sidelites and Transoms
Count sidelite and transom as separate openings. Window opening rate applies with same scrutiny from window doctrine.

### 12.5 Garage Entry Doors (Fire-Rated)
Same exterior rules apply. Tape door label on hinge side to avoid covering with paint. Same scrutiny as other exterior-grade doors.
