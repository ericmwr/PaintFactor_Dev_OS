## 1. Landscaping Protection During Spray Operations

### The Breathability vs. Containment Tradeoff
- Polyethylene (plastic) sheeting: impenetrable barrier against paint liquid, ideal for high-volume spray
- **Risk**: Plastic traps heat and blocks gas exchange — plants can reach lethal temperatures within hours (greenhouse effect) in hot weather
- **Solution**: Use breathable drop cloths or canvas for extended coverage; limit plastic to active spray windows only

### Protection Methods by Plant Type

| Plant Type | Method | Duration Limit |
|-----------|--------|---------------|
| Ground cover / grass | Canvas drop cloths or breathable tarps | Remove same day |
| Shrubs (< 4 ft) | Poly sheeting tent with stakes; remove between spray sessions | Max 4-6 hours in sun |
| Large shrubs / small trees | Pull back from structure with soft ties; drape with breathable fabric | Duration of spray phase |
| Mature trees | Not typically covered; protect trunk base with canvas | N/A |
| Delicate/specialty plants | Relocate in containers if possible; pre-water to reduce stress | N/A |

### Post-Painting Plant Care
- Gently rinse foliage with water to remove dust/residue
- Helps cool plants that experienced heat accumulation under covers

## 2. Hardscape Protection

### Material-Specific Risks and Methods

| Hardscape Material | Stain Risk | Recommended Protection |
|-------------------|-----------|----------------------|
| Broom-finish concrete | High (porous) | Absorbent runners; 6-mil poly with canvas overlap |
| Decorative pavers | Very high (joints absorb) | Reinforced poly sheeting with taped edges |
| Natural stone | High (texture traps paint) | Ram Board or heavy-duty temporary floor protection |
| Asphalt driveways | Moderate | Canvas drop cloths (breathable, non-slip) |

### Safety Note
Standard plastic sheeting on hardscapes is a **safety hazard** — becomes "slippery as ice" when wet with paint or morning dew. Professional contractors use absorbent floor mats (e.g., Abzorb, G-Floor) with:
- Non-penetrable vinyl/rubber backing
- Polypropylene/felt top layer for traction
- Wicking action prevents paint tracking

## 3. HVAC, Electrical, and Fixture Protection

### HVAC Condenser Protocol ("Shutdown and Shield")
1. **Power down** at local disconnect switch before spraying in vicinity
2. **Never cover with non-porous material while operating** — compressor will overheat and fail
3. Use breathable mesh covers or temporary plywood "lean-tos" that block paint drift while allowing air circulation
4. For long-term protection in coastal/industrial environments: Heresite protective coatings on coils and fins

### Electrical Infrastructure

| Component | Method | Key Requirement |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|
| Electrical meter | Precision mask glass face and perimeter seal with 7-mil red poly tape | Must remain readable |
| Outlet covers | SmartGuard covers or precision tape | NEC/OSHA: non-conductive coatings must not interfere with connections |
| Electrical panels | Do not paint; mask completely | Access must remain clear |

### Exterior Light Fixtures ("Removal and Bagging")
1. **Power verification**: Test and power down circuit at breaker
2. **Removal**: Detach fixture from mounting plate to paint surface behind (prevents "halo" effect)
3. **Encapsulation** (if removal not possible): Pre-taped poly "fixture bag" over decorative glass and sockets

### Plumbing and Ventilation Vents
- Must be protected from paint but **never fully obstructed** (sewer gas / CO buildup risk)
- Cut "X" slits in tarps for vent protrusion
- Seal vent base with waterproof tape to prevent drips into roof penetration

## 4. Window and Door Masking (Whole-House Spray)

### Modern Masking Systems

| Product | Material | Thickness | Application |
|---------|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Advanced masking film | HDPE | 0.4 mil | Windows and doors; static-cling to glass |
| Pre-taped poly | LDPE | 0.7 mil | Detailed trim protection; door surrounds |
| Masking paper | Kraft paper | 3-5 mil | Soffits and fascia; resists bleed-through |
| Liquid mask | Polymer dispersion | Peelable | Multi-pane glass; architectural details |

### Production Method
- Hand-held maskers simultaneously apply tape + pre-folded masking film
- Single technician can mask a standard window in under 5 minutes
- Film is statically charged — clings to glass, resists wind and sprayer force

### Liquid Mask Caution
- Highly effective in controlled environments
- Can trap moisture or become difficult to remove if left in UV > few days

### "Trim First" / Reverse Masking Protocol
1. Paint trim and fascia first
2. "Reverse mask" trim with 12-inch masking paper/film "skirt"
3. Spray siding with spray shields at trim-siding intersection for secondary protection

## 5. Roofing Protection

### Asphalt Shingle Protection
- Shingles coated with mineral granules (UV + fire resistance)
- Paint bonds deeply with granules — **cannot be pressure washed or scraped without voiding warranty**
- Solvent-based removers dissolve asphalt substrate
- **Remediation ("Fogging" method)**: Color-matched paint thinned 1:5 to 1:7 (paint:water), lightly misted to hide overspray

### Protection by Roof Type

| Roof Type | Protection Method | Special Considerations |
|-----------|------------------|----------------------|
| Asphalt shingles | Heavy tarps secured with "Wrap and Roll" (edges around 2x4, screwed to deck) | Wind-flapping damages shingles |
| Clay/concrete tile | 10-mil poly tarps + walk-boards/foam pads | Weight can crack tiles |
| Metal roofing | Magnetic tarp holders or residue-free adhesive tape | Slippery surface; scratch-prone |
| Flat/membrane | Tarp with weighted edges | Avoid puncturing membrane |

### Vent Penetrations
- Cut "X" slits in tarps for roof vents/chimneys
- Seal base with waterproof tape
- Never obstruct vents for extended periods

## 6. Standard Labor Time: Setup and Teardown

### Protection as Percentage of Total Labor
- Protection/masking typically accounts for **30-40% of total man-hours** on exterior spray projects
- Labor typically 50-70% of total project cost
- Preparation and protection often account for 50-85% of total project labor hours

### Typical 5-Day Residential Schedule (2,000-2,500 SF home)

| Day | Phase | Protection Activities |
|-----|-------|----------------------|
| 1 | Cleansing + initial setup | Power wash; first layer ground protection; identify repairs |
| 2 | Preparation + repair | Scraping, sanding, caulking; surface ready for bond |
| 3 | Masking + priming | Encapsulate windows/doors; shield HVAC/electrical; spot prime — **most critical protection day** |
| 4 | Primary spray | High-volume first/second coats; active shielding with handheld shields |
| 5 | Teardown + cleanup | Remove all masking/tarps; final touch-ups; site inspection; customer walkthrough |

## 7. Vehicle, Driveway, and Neighbor Protection

### Vehicle Protection
- Relocate all vehicles from spray zone when possible
- Vehicles that cannot be moved: 0.5-mil poly vehicle covers (professional grade)
- **Overspray remediation on vehicles**: Automotive clay bar treatment + full polish and wax (lifts paint particles from clear coat safely)

### Neighbor Property
- Monitor wind speed with handheld anemometer — halt operations above 10 mph
- Active spray shielding at property boundaries
- Pre-notification of adjacent property owners is standard professional practice
- **Liability**: Overspray damage to neighboring property is a significant legal/financial risk

### Wind Management During Spray
- Acceptable range: 1.2-9.3 mph
- Operations halt above 10 mph
- Use low-pressure tips and mechanical spray shields in 6-9 mph range
