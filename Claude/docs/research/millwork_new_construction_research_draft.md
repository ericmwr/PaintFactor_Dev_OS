## 2. Substrate Classification

### 2.1 MDF (Medium Density Fiberboard)

- **Composition**: Sawdust, wood chips, small wood shavings, and resin compressed under high pressure
- **Characteristics**:
  - Uniform, no grain pattern or knots
  - High face strength, resistant to surface damage
  - Stable - does not expand/contract as aggressively as solid wood
  - Lacks natural rigidity ("floppy" in long lengths)
- **Primer Requirements**:
  - Shellac or oil-based primer for initial seal (prevents fiber raise)
  - Edges require dedicated MDF primer or shellac-based sealer
  - Sand with 220-grit after seal coat, apply second primer coat
- **Known Issues**:
  - Edge absorption and fiber raise (porous edges absorb unevenly)
  - Highly susceptible to moisture - swells like sponge if paint film breached
  - Risk of "pithy" texture if water-based products applied to raw edges

### 2.2 Finger-Joint Pine (FJP)

- **Composition**: Small, defect-free pieces of pine joined with interlocking finger joints and high-strength adhesives
- **Characteristics**:
  - Visible grain and joints
  - Superior nail-holding capacity and rigidity vs MDF
  - Compresses around fasteners (easier filling vs MDF which "puckers")
  - Moderate moisture sensitivity
- **Primer Requirements**:
  - High-quality stain-blocking primer (resins may bleed through water-based)
  - Factory primers often thin - apply additional high-build undercoat
- **Known Issues**:
  - Joint telegraphing through finish over time (seasonal movement)
  - Grain raise if water-based coatings on unprimed wood

### 2.3 Primed Poplar (Solid Hardwood)

- **Characteristics**: Fine, uniform grain; highly stable; low moisture sensitivity
- **Primer Requirements**: Standard high-quality primer
- **Known Issues**: Higher material cost
- **Benefits**: Exceptional paint surface

### 2.4 Composite/PVC Trim

- **Characteristics**: Synthetic, moisture-proof
- **Primer Requirements**: Bonding primer may be needed for adhesion
- **Known Issues**: Requires compatible coatings; expansion/contraction differs from wood

### 2.5 Factory-Primed vs Field-Primed

| Condition | Additional Prep Required | Primer Coat Required |
|-----------|-------------------------|---------------------|
| Factory primed (intact) | Light sand, inspect for holidays | Often yes - factory primers are thin/transit protection only |
| Factory primed (damaged) | Spot prime bare areas, sand blend | Yes - full coat recommended |
| Unprimed | Full prep per substrate type | Yes - seal coat + build coat |

## 3. Surface Preparation Requirements

### 3.1 Preparation Matrix by Substrate

| Substrate | Sanding | Filling | Caulking | Cleaning | Priming |
|-----------|---------|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| MDF | 120-grit edges, 220-grit faces | Nail holes, defects | All joints | Dust removal | Shellac/oil seal + latex build |
| FJP | 150-180 grit | Nail holes, joint voids | All joints | Dust removal | Stain-blocking primer |
| Solid wood | 150-180 grit | Nail holes, defects | All joints | Dust removal | Standard primer |
| Composite | Per mfr spec | Minimal | Joints only | Dust/contaminant removal | Bonding primer if required |

### 3.2 PCA Standard P14: Levels of Surface Preparation

| Level | Description | Defect Threshold | Typical Use |
|-------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| Level 1 | Basic | Major defects only | Budget/rental |
| Level 2 | Standard | Visible defects | Builder grade |
| Level 3 | Premium | Minor defects | Custom homes |
| Level 4 | Supreme | Any defect > 1/32" filled and sanded | High-end, semi-gloss/gloss finishes |

> **Note**: Level 4 (Supreme) required for high-gloss or semi-gloss enamels - these sheens emphasize even smallest substrate irregularities.

### 3.3 Joint Treatment Standards

- All trim joints require caulking for seamless appearance
- Use painter's caulk (not silicone) for paintability
- PCA Standard P11: Do not apply caulk over contaminated surfaces or during extreme humidity

### 3.4 Nail Hole & Defect Filling

- Use high-quality wood filler or lightweight spackle
- Fill flush, allow full cure before sanding
- Sand with 220-grit for smooth blend
- FJP compresses around fasteners - easier fill than MDF

## 4. Primer Systems

### 4.1 Primer Selection Matrix

| Substrate | Recommended Primer Type | Purpose | Notes |
|-----------|------------------------|---------|-------|
| MDF (edges) | Shellac-based or oil-based | Seal porous edges, prevent fiber raise | Water-based causes swelling |
| MDF (faces) | High-build latex after seal | Build film, level surface | Sand between coats |
| FJP | Stain-blocking primer | Block resin bleed | High-quality required |
| Solid wood | Standard primer-sealer | Seal, tooth, stain block | Match to topcoat system |
| Factory primed | High-build undercoat | Build film thickness | Factory primer often insufficient |

### 4.2 Primer Functions (3 requirements)

1. **Seal the substrate** - Prevent uneven absorption
2. **Provide tooth** - Mechanical adhesion for topcoat
3. **Block stains/tannins** - Prevent bleed-through

### 4.3 High-Build Primers

- Fill minor surface scratches
- Create smooth underlayment
- Essential for achieving Level 4 finish quality

### 4.4 Shellac-Based Primers

**Use when:**
- Sealing MDF edges
- Knot sealing on wood
- Tannin blocking (oak, cedar, redwood)
- Odor blocking

## 5. Finish Coat Systems

### 5.1 Product Categories

| Coating Type | Benefits | Limitations | Recommended Use |
|--------------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Acrylic Latex | Fast dry, low odor | Lower hardness, poor leveling | General trim in budget projects |
| Waterborne Alkyd | Excellent leveling, hard finish | Longer cure time than latex | High-end residential trim |
| Urethane Enamel | Superior durability, chemical resistance | More expensive per gallon | Kitchen cabinets, high-traffic trim |
| Epoxy (2-Pack) | Extreme hardness, chemical resistant | Complex mixing, limited pot life | Commercial or heavy-use areas |

### 5.2 Sheen Selection

| Sheen | Typical Use | Durability | Touch-Up | Notes |
|-------|-------------|------------|----------|-------|
| Satin | Standard trim | Good | Moderate | Most forgiving |
| Semi-gloss | Traditional trim, kitchens/baths | Very good | Moderate | Shows surface defects |
| Gloss | High-end, specialty | Excellent | Difficult | Requires Level 4 prep |

### 5.3 Self-Leveling Properties

- Premium acrylic-urethane enamels designed to flow out after application
- Eliminates brush marks and orange peel texture
- Critical for spray application finish quality

## 8. Common Defects & Mitigation

| Defect | Cause | Prevention | Quality Tier Impact |
|--------|-------|------------|---------------------|
| Grain raise | Water-based product on raw MDF/wood | Use shellac/oil seal coat first | All tiers - must prevent |
| Telegraphing (joints) | FJP joint movement, insufficient build | High-build primer, multiple coats | QT4+ unacceptable |
| Orange peel | Spray technique, material viscosity | Proper atomization, self-leveling enamel | QT3 acceptable, QT4+ unacceptable |
| Brush marks | Manual application, poor leveling | Use self-leveling enamel, proper technique | QT2-3 acceptable, QT4+ spray only |
| Sags/runs | Over-application, vertical surfaces | Proper mil thickness, technique | All tiers - defect |
| Flash (sheen variation) | Uneven absorption, spot priming | Full seal coat, uniform build | QT3+ unacceptable |
| Adhesion failure | Contamination, incompatible coatings | Proper prep, compatible systems | All tiers - failure |
| Fiber raise (MDF) | Water in primer penetrating edges | Shellac/oil edge seal | All tiers - must prevent |

### 9.2 PCA P1 - The 39-Inch Rule

A surface is properly painted when uniform in appearance, color, texture, and sheen, free of defects when viewed:
- Without magnification
- At distance of 39 inches (1 meter) or more
- Under finished lighting conditions
- From normal viewing position

### 9.3 PCA P5 - Benchmark Samples

- Create representative section finished to specified quality
- Must be approved by owner/GC before proceeding
- Establishes achievable quality standard for entire project
